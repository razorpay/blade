# Ray

Ray is Razorpay's AI mascot: a small character that sits on the chat input, idles, watches what's typed, looks at the pointer, flips a coin when clicked, and plays the states of an answer while it's being worked on (searching, calling a tool, writing a plan, working). It's drawn by Ray Studio's engine, the same code the dashboard prototype (flow 9) and the Ray Studio films page play, so what's tuned there is what ships here.

## Design

- Ray Studio (ray-spec): the engine, the canon scripts and the films. The user-tuned values every film reads are exported with it.
- Figma: watch frames 45:805, nudge 53:918, hide 46:851 (the typing and hop moves), the films' frames (searching 360:673, cash 521:1033, reading box 521:1113, …).

## API

```jsx
import { Ray, ChatInput } from '@razorpay/blade/components';

const inputRef = useRef(null);

// on the chat input: dashes in on the landing, idles, watches the caret while typing (and nudges when it stops), hops off when a question is sent
<Box position="relative">
  <Box position="absolute" bottom="100%" right="spacing.10">
    <Ray entrance="dash" idle="rotate" watchedInputRef={inputRef} isVisible={!isAnswering} />
  </Box>
  <ChatInput ref={inputRef} … />
</Box>

// in the answer's row: its states, handing over mid-move; at rest between them; drops out when it's done, and the row closes up
<Ray state={step} idle="rest" isVisible={!isDone} shouldUnmountWhenHidden shouldReactToPointer={false} />

// back on the chat input: how the answer ended (once), offline with the app, a mic while the page listens (its own stream)
<Ray idle="rotate" watchedInputRef={inputRef} isVisible={!isAnswering} outcome={outcome} isOffline={isOffline}
  isListening={isListening} voiceStream={voiceStream} />
```

### Props

| Prop | Type | Default | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `entrance` | `'none' \| 'dash'` | `'none'` | How he arrives on mount. `dash` is the dashboard's landing: he dashes in along the surface from its left (from 200px inside the input's left edge when `watchedInputRef` is given), brakes, hops into place and starts his idle. Typing, the hop or an answer cuts it short. Skipped when he starts hidden, with reduced motion, and when his idle is Sleep (he's found asleep). |
| `state` | `'idle' \| 'searching' \| 'thinking' \| 'reading' \| 'calling' \| 'writing' \| 'working'` | `'idle'` | What he's doing. The answer states are his films (`thinking` the same magnifying glass as `searching`; `reading` the box with the papers); going from one to the next hands him over mid-move. |
| `idle` | `'rotate' \| 'rest' \| 'coffee' \| 'look' \| 'cash' \| 'reading' \| 'sleep' \| 'time-of-day'` | `'look'` | His idle while `state` is `idle`. A named idle renders the same every time; `rotate` and `time-of-day` are opt-in (they choose for him and use `localStorage`). `rotate` takes the next of coffee → look → cash → reading → sleep on each page load (as the dashboard does). `rest` keeps him at rest. `time-of-day` picks one by the viewer's clock as each Ray appears, and checks it again every half minute while he's up (an idle the hour has since ruled out gives way to another): the nightcap (Sleep) only 9pm–6am and likeliest then (half the loads), never Coffee then; Coffee likeliest 7–10am (half the loads); evenly otherwise, and not the last load's idle again unless it's the hour's favourite (the last pick is remembered in `localStorage`, `blade-ray-idle-time`). `sleep` is the sleep cycle: found asleep with the nightcap on, he wakes himself after about 10 s, looks around awake, and nods off again after a minute with nobody near; a pointer near him or a click wakes him, and the cap stays on for the visit (his flip is then the toss). |
| `size` | `'small' \| 'medium'` | `'medium'` | His box, the room he takes in the layout: 38 × 31.51px (a side panel) or 48 × 39.8px (the dashboard and chat). |
| `isVisible` | `boolean` | `true` | `false`: he hops up and drops behind the surface he sits on; `true` again brings him back up out of it (as Blade's other animated surfaces, `isVisible`). Whatever he's playing finishes its bit first. |
| `shouldUnmountWhenHidden` | `boolean` | `false` | With `isVisible={false}`, his box is removed too once he has hopped off, so the row he sat in closes up (as Fade and Move); `isVisible` back lays it out again and he comes up out of the surface. Off by default because the layout shifts (the PR review, 2026-11-10: a hidden answer-row Ray left an empty box). |
| `watchedInputRef` | `RefObject<HTMLElement>` | – | The field he watches (named for that, not `inputRef`, which in Blade is a component's own input, as TimePicker's; the PR review): with text and focus he leans out to the caret and follows it (body along the four watch frames, eyes on the caret). After 10 s with no typing he nudges (canon Nudge: a look straight out, then back to the caret); typing during it takes him straight back to watching. There's no prop for the nudge: it comes with `watchedInputRef`. |
| `shouldReactToPointer` | `boolean` | `true` | The hover look (he looks at the pointer anywhere on the page) and the coin flip on click (click again while a coin is up for another). It works on every idle, and while he watches what's typed (the click takes the field's focus: he flips from where he is and settles back into his idle). Look around and Cash flip at once. Reading box and Coffee first finish what they're doing; Coffee's sip and Cash's reload always play out first. Sleep wakes him first. |
| `accessibilityLabel` | `string` | – | Exposes him as an image with this label; leave unset when he's decorative. |
| `isOffline` | `boolean` | `false` | Offline: grey, his eyes twitching, a glitch every few seconds (Ray Studio's droop, malfunction). Back to `false`, he jumps back online. Wins over everything else. |
| `isListening` | `boolean` | `false` | A mic whips out and he listens (Ray Studio's mic); back to `false`, it whips back in. |
| `voiceStream` | `MediaStream` | – | The page's own voice input, for the mic's level. He reads its level once a frame and never opens a mic himself. |
| `outcome` | `'success' \| 'error'` | – | How the answer ended, played once when set: canon Success (a jump with confetti, a little dance) or droop's sad one-shot. Waits for an answer's states to end and for him to be back on his surface. Set again after clearing it to play again. |
| `testID`, styled props | | | As other Blade components. |

### Naming

- **`shouldReactToPointer`, not `isInteractive` or `onClick`.** Blade makes a component interactive from its handler (a StepItem with `onClick` or `href`, StepGroup's decisions), and no Blade component has a public `isInteractive`. Ray's pointer look and coin flip are a delight with nothing for the app to do: no handler to derive them from, and an `onClick` would ask for keyboard access to a click that does nothing. So the prop is named for what it does, on by default as the dashboard has him, and off where he only shows what's happening (an answer's row). It has no keyboard equivalent, as it carries no information. (The PR reviews, 2026-11-10 to -16.)
- **`state` and `idle`, kept as they are.** The reviews asked to rename `state="idle"` or merge it with `idle`, and to move `rotate` and `time-of-day` out of `idle` into a prop of their own. Kept, deliberately: `state` is what the answer is doing and `idle` is what he does when nothing is, so a chat-input Ray needs only `idle` and an answer row only `state` (with `idle="rest"`), and one `idle` prop covers both a named idle and a chooser without a second prop that only matters for two of its values. The choosers' `localStorage` use is opt-in and documented. (Decided 2026-11-10, recorded 2026-11-16.)

- **`thinking` and `searching`: two names, one film** (the magnifying glass; the user, 2026-11-24: "thinking is the magnifying glass"). An answer's plan names its steps (thinking, reading, …): an app passes them as they are, with no mapping of its own. `searching` stays as it shipped. `reading` (the user: "reading is the one with the box and the papers coming out") is Reading box's film, an idle too, here as an answer state.
- **`isOffline`, `isListening`, `voiceStream`, `outcome`: #4100's RazorSense 2.0 names** (decided 2026-11-23, the user: "makes sense"), so a page passes one set to both and they show the same thing. `outcome`, not more `state` values: it's how the answer ended, played once on his seat, not something he stays in; `state` stays what the answer is doing. `voiceStream`, not a mic of his own: Blade's ChatInput has no voice input, so the page owns the mic (and its permission prompt) and hands its stream in; each component only reads levels from it, inside its own frame loop, with no React update per frame.

His box is his rest footprint and never changes size (unless `shouldUnmountWhenHidden` removes it once he's hidden). Everything he does around it (props, a coin in the air, the hop) draws outside it without affecting layout, so the parent must not clip it. To the right of his box and below it, his drawing stops 1 and 0.75 of his widths out (what he draws there reaches 0.38 and 0.27, measured over every idle, flip, film, the hop and the dash). So in a scrolling parent (a chat's message list) he adds at most that much scroll room, not his films' whole stage (the PR review, 2026-11-10). Above him and to his left it runs on, since nothing scrolls there.

## How it's built

- `engine/` is **generated** by `ray-spec/studio/blade/export.mjs` from Ray Studio: the engine (`rayCore.ts`), its stateful helpers (flip, nightcap, wardrobe, tour), the canon scripts, the films and the tunes. Never edit it here: change Ray Studio and export again.
- Each film keeps its page's own code and runs against `FilmHost`, a stand-in page: its canvases, a message channel for the `ready / start / leave / left / done / …` protocol, its saved tune, the stage's size. No iframes.
- `RayPlayer` is his own Ray for what has no film: rest, Look around, the hop (canon Hide), the typing watch, the hover look, and the coin flip.
- `RayEntrance` is the landing dash, on a canvas of its own wide enough for the run; his own Ray takes him from rest on the frame it ends.
- `RayMount` directs. Answer films hand over mid-move, and while the next film hasn't drawn yet, his own Ray holds the pose the last one left him in. A film takes him from rest and gives him back at rest, where both draw the same frame. A film loaded ahead (the answer's next, a state preloaded) draws one frame unseen as it loads, then sleeps (`FilmHost.rehearse`): what a film's first frame does only once (its canvases' first draws, its WebGL lens drawn the first time, when Windows' Direct3D builds the shader program) is done while it waits, not in the frame it takes him in. And Searching starts from the pass it worked out as it loaded rather than working out the same one again (seed 1). Its lens shader is built in the background where the browser can (`KHR_parallel_shader_compile`: Chromium, WebKit) and set up once the browser says it's built; a lens needed before then waits, as it always did (on Windows the build had moved to the approach: one 63–111 ms frame). Searching's load is split into steps, a task each (its script; its first pass worked out; its WebGL lens begun; then ready), and the coin flip's physics are made ahead only for a Ray that can be clicked (`shouldReactToPointer`): a button's Ray never flips (the device test: as the pointer neared a button, the load was one 47–57 ms task and the flip's warm-up another 56 ms on a Windows laptop). Its first pass is one `simulate()` call, which can't be split without changing ray-core. Its WebGL context is made a quarter second after it's ready (made as a button's own hover effect starts on the GPU, it waited 9–17 ms; after it, 2), or at once if the glass needs it first. A Ray that appears later can have its code and a state's film fetched ahead while the page is idle (`loadRay`, internal: code only, nothing made), so a button's first hover doesn't wait on a download (the device test: on a page's first Investigate button the film was still loading as the hover began). Warming `simulate()` ahead didn't make the first pass any quicker, so it isn't done. Together they took a first hover's slowest frame from 25–40 ms to under 10 ms on a Mac (Chromium, Firefox), where a Windows GTX 1650 showed 83–97 ms (the device test, 2026-11-17). A film still loading never holds him up: if what's asked of him changes first (he's hidden, typing starts, the answer or his idle moves on), it's let go and he goes on at once; only a film he still wants is waited for, up to 8 s before he rests instead (the bot's review, 2026-11-16). An idle film plays its exit first (1.6× quick for typing and the hop). The flip always plays out. One loop draws everything, and it pauses while he's off-screen. Every Ray on a page shares that loop and one set of page listeners: each frame, every Ray first reads what it needs measured (where it is, the caret), then every Ray draws, so no read comes after another's writes (the performance review, 2026-11-13). The loop paces itself (`rayPace.ts`): each drawn frame's work is timed, and when it takes more than a quarter of the time between frames every Ray draws every other frame (then every third, …), never under 30 fps, going back up once it fits in a fifth. A fast machine draws every frame; a slow one keeps the page responsive with his look unchanged, only less smooth (the performance re-review, 2026-11-16, chosen over a flat 60 fps cap or dropping the glow, which costs nothing now that his canvas is cropped).
- His own Ray's canvas covers only the part of the stage he ever draws on (about twice what was measured over his idles, typing, the flips, the hop and the sleep cycle): 8.5% of the stage's pixels, drawn on the same pixels as before. Each film's canvases cover only the part of the stage it ever draws on too (`FILM_CROP`, from each film's measured reach, about 1.5 times it; 4–15% of the stage, against the whole stage before): the film lays him out from the whole stage and draws on that part (ray-core's `stageCrop`, given to it as `window.rayCrop`), on the same pixels (razorpay/blade#4116, 2026-11-16). A film he's done with frees its canvases, its engine layers and its WebGL context at once, not at the next garbage collection.
- His floor is the top of what he sits on. The stage is cut there only while something should go behind it: the hop down and a coin falling past. The rest of the time nothing's cut, so a prop he holds out over the box's edge (Coffee's cup) or below the answer's line (the magnifier's handle) stays whole.
- The coin flip's physics are made once per page in idle time (`flipPrepare`), from his rest; the pose he's really in is carried in as a gap that dies away on a critically damped spring as he squashes, and the gap from the flick's pose dies away the same way as his idle takes him back. Simulating it on the click cost 11–26 ms, one to three dropped frames at 120 Hz.
- The nightcap is the sleep idle's (the user, 2026-11-18): it goes on as the sleep cycle starts and he wears it while Sleep is his idle, asleep or dozing, and in an answer's films meanwhile (as flow 9's do). As another idle starts (Coffee when `time-of-day` rules Sleep out at 6am, an app's new `idle`, or `rest`), it comes off, on him and in that idle's film. It goes on and off at once, as an idle starts, as it always went on.
- **One thing at a time** (the PR update plan, 2026-11-23): hidden; offline, which wins over everything else (the mic goes at once, as in flow 10.5; an answer's film plays its exit first); an answer's state; the outcome, once the answer's states are over and he's back on his seat; the mic; watching what's typed; his idle. Each new state has its own entry and exit, the film's own, and turns back mid-way from where he is (offline again as he jumps back online; the mic again as it goes in). From an idle film, offline, the mic and an outcome take him with a send's quick hand-over (its exit at the hop's 1.6x), as flow 10.5 does (mal9, mic9, end9: "his idle eases to rest (a send's quick hand-over)").
- **An outcome set again while one plays, plays next** (the fresh-eyes review, 2026-11-24): each time `outcome` is set is counted, and the one playing is over at its end only if it wasn't set again meanwhile. "Play again" mid-Success plays it again after it; the error set mid-Success cuts it short and plays. A new director (his box back with `shouldUnmountWhenHidden`, reduced motion changed) doesn't play an outcome that has already had its turn.
- **The page's audio rests till a Ray listens** (the fresh-eyes review): it's made ahead, in idle time, as a `voiceStream` is handed over (a page's first audio context is slow to make), but suspended at once unless one is listening, resumed as the mic plays and suspended again when no Ray listens.
- **A film let go of is handed to the next Ray** (filmPool; #4101's button sweep, 2026-11-24). An Investigate button's Ray is made on each hover, and his Searching film was made with him each time: its script, its first pass (51 ms at 4x CPU) and its WebGL lens. Now a film a Ray lets go of is parked (asleep, out of the page) for 1.5 s and the next Ray wanting it takes it, ready. One of each film and two in all are parked, never one that failed a frame or whose lens was lost, a lensed one only while the page holds 3 lenses at most, and a Ray takes a parked one before making one. A parked film makes no WebGL context (Searching's lens comes 250 ms after it's ready: one parked sooner makes it once a Ray has it), and every parked film is let go as the tab is hidden (its timers would be throttled, stretching the 1.5 s) or the page goes (the second review). #4101's sweep (16 rows) made 12 WebGL contexts instead of 19 (at 1x and 4x CPU); the most live at once went from 4 to 5 (a parked lens beside the hovers' stages, for 1.5 s at most). A film's messages carry a turn both ways, so nothing it said for the last Ray reaches the next. The first pass itself can't be split from Blade (the films call the engine's `simulate` synchronously): with the pool it runs once a page, not once a hover; splitting it is a Ray Studio change.
- **On a GPU that draws on the CPU, his films get no WebGL** (the user's rule, 2026-11-17: optimise without compromising the look, with fallbacks only where the hardware really can't keep up; RazorSense shows its stills there). A software renderer (RazorSense's list, `RzpGlass/gpuPatterns.ts`: SwiftShader, llvmpipe, Microsoft Basic Render…) is found on the first WebGL context a film asks for, asked with `failIfMajorPerformanceCaveat` so it usually makes none. Searching then plays with its ring and 2D frame and no lens (its look without WebGL, otherwise the same), and the lens's program is never built on the CPU (SwiftShader: 276–426 ms long tasks as an answer's Ray started searching). His 2D drawing (his own Ray and the films) keeps animating: on SwiftShader, with the lens gone, the answer row held 35–40 fps and flow 10.5's chat 28–43 fps a step, with no long tasks (9, 1.4–1.8 s, before). What's left is the page's first WebGL context on SwiftShader (816 ms), made by the check itself when nothing else on the page has made one (RazorSense's own check makes it first where it's on the page).
- **The new states' films** are Ray Studio's: `droop` (offline is its malfunction, `outcome="error"` its sad one-shot) and `mic`, exported like the others and loaded only when first needed. The mic film's own microphone path is left out of the export: the director feeds it the level from `voiceStream` (one analyser read into one buffer a frame, sent only when it changes). **Success** has no film: it's canon Success (Figma 81:1479, with the user's tune; `release: 0` on every key since 2026-11-24, so it plays as flow 10.5's V17 Success: no squeeze kick, peak 1.40 widths, no mid-air squash; its confetti pieces 20% bigger since 2026-11-24, the user, on its own confetti effect: size 27.48, was the engine's 22.9) on his own Ray, so the same engine draws it (one engine, as decided on 3 Nov). Nothing new happens in a frame he's seen in (the device test's rule): each film loads in steps and says it's ready after the last (as Searching does: their first mount was a 47–80 ms task with a CPU 4x slower, now 2–6 ms); Success is worked out in idle time once `outcome` asks for it; and the audio for the voice's level is set up in idle time as `voiceStream` is handed over, one audio context for every Ray on the page (a page's first one took 124 ms with a CPU 4x slower).
- **The hops are each Ray's own entry and exit, timed as flow 10.5 has them** (measured there on 2026-11-23: its hide 465 ms to out of sight, its show 581 ms to landed; canon hide's are 467 and 583 at 1x). The chat input's Ray drops off as the question goes, and comes back up at 1.2x; with an outcome waiting, his hop ends as he lands and the outcome starts there. The answer row's Ray jumps quicker both ways, at 1.7x (the user, 2026-11-24: "the answer jumps should be faster, go with 1.7x if 10.5 is using 2x"; flow 10.5's rowHop plays at 2x): up out of his row (canon hide's second half, rowHop's window), his first film settling from that pose over 320 ms; and, as the answer's done and he's hidden, down from the moment his film's exit is cut, 20 ms after its prop is put away (or once he's at rest), read off the film's own handle as flow 10.5 reads it (600 ms into the laptop's exit there).
- Nothing heavy loads up front: the director, his own Ray, the engine and canon (about 57 KB gzipped) load with his first render (`import()`), so an app that imports Blade's components without rendering him never downloads them (the PR review, 2026-11-10); his box is laid out meanwhile from `rayBox.ts`, which a test keeps equal to the engine's sizes. Films load when first used: 13 KB of shared film helpers with the first film, then 5–15 KB per film. A film he's done with is freed (an answer's past states, an idle he's moved on from), so at most his idle's film and the answer's now and next are loaded.

## Accessibility

- Decorative by default (`aria-hidden`). `accessibilityLabel` makes him `role="status"` while `state` is an answer state (as DotLoader is, so a label that changes with the state can be announced; screen readers announce a live region's text more reliably than its label, as with DotLoader) and `role="img"` at rest.
- The coin flip is a pointer delight with no keyboard equivalent, since it carries no information.
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: one still frame, no animation: at rest, or the state's own still (offline, error, the mic: drawn by its film, loaded for it; Success: his dance, happy eyes). An outcome's still stays about as long as it would play. Turned on or off while he's up, he follows it (a new director takes over, still or moving).
- `isOffline` and `outcome` aren't announced by Ray (he's decorative unless labelled): the page says what happened in text too.

## Platform

Web only. `Ray.native.tsx` throws "not yet implemented": his engine and films draw on web canvases.

## Open questions

- **Bundle size** (gzipped, esbuild with code splitting, 2026-11-24). With Blade's components only his component loads (8.9 KB, mostly code every component shares). With his first render, 60.3 KB (director, player, engine, canon, tunes; 55.8 before the new states). Each film loads only when first needed: 4.0–16.0 KB (droop 16.0, mic 8.6), plus shared film code (wardrobe 14.0, flip 5.7, nightcap 3.4) with the first film that uses it. `thinking` and `reading` add no chunk. The new films' tunes (3.2 KB minified) load with the engine, as every film's do: the export writes them as one file.
- **A click mid-sip or mid-cash-run** costs one or two frames at the click: those films re-plan their bit then (a re-simulation). Moving the engine's physics to a worker would remove it.
- **`size` tokens.** Two sizes match today's dashboard (48) and sidekick (38). Investigate's 49.5px button isn't covered.
- **Not in v1:**
  - the wardrobe
  - the "Thinking (drop)" film
  - droop's other looks (offline asleep, the red error shake): offline is the malfunction only, as the user asked
- **Success in dark** is the same motion on the dark ground, in his own dark look (approved by the user on 2026-11-24, from flow 10.5's V17 Success played on the dark ground).
- **`idle="rotate"` stores a counter** in `localStorage` (`blade-ray-idle`) to step the cycle per page load. Without storage, it starts at a random idle.
